## 2. Fractal Tensors FFE (Fractal Field Entities)
2.1 General Definition

Fractal tensors FFE constitute the fundamental unit of intelligent computation within the Aurora model. An FFE tensor is a structured vector of discrete numerical components divided into dimensions, where each element represents an essential component of information and its operation.
Unlike traditional vectors, FFE tensors are semantic: every number within them carries a specific meaning inside the system, rather than an arbitrary numerical value. In this way, the tensor not only stores data but also describes how that data must be interpreted and operated upon.

## 2.2 Discrete and Quantum-Like Nature

Fractal FFE tensors do not admit infinitesimal values. Their components are quantized, defined within a discrete range that reflects the granular nature of both physical energy and information.
The minimal vector possible in an intelligent system is composed of three dimensions, each represented by trits (an extension of the bit that admits three states: 0, 1, and null).
This three-dimensional vector becomes the basic unit of intelligent processing within Aurora, and its structure is self-contained, as it includes both information and the rule of operation.

## 2.3 Meaning of the Three Dimensions

Each of the three dimensions within a tensor fulfills a specific function:

First dimension – Form: Contains the information or “shape” representing the content of the tensor.

Second dimension – Function: Indicates which active dimension contains the relevant datum, or how the information should be interpreted within the set.

Third dimension – Structure: Represents the partial instruction or operational tendency that the tensor must execute or transfer to the system.

## 2.4 Contextual Dynamics

In a fractal system, the role of each dimension is not fixed. Depending on the interactions of the tensor with surrounding tensors, each dimension may modify its function: a value acting as data in one context may behave as an operation or relational indicator in another.
Thus, FFE tensors are not statically ordered between “value” and “function.” Their behavior depends on the semantic environment or context, allowing them to adapt, cooperate, and reorganize dynamically—just as occurs in living systems, quantum processes, or natural languages.

## 2.5 Relationship Between Hierarchical Levels

One of the most important characteristics of the Aurora system is the coherent and dynamic relationship between the hierarchical levels of fractal FFE tensors.
Each higher-level dimension contains and governs three subordinate dimensions, establishing a fractal 3³ structure (3×9×27), where each upper-level dimension decomposes into three lower-level dimensions.
